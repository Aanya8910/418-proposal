Cutting the Verify: In-Pass Draft Truncation for Mixture-of-Experts Speculative Decoding

Anya Shankar (anyashan) · Aanya Kochhar (aanyak) · 15-418, Fall 2026

Summary

We set out to make speculative-decoding verification cheaper for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models by deciding during the forward pass which trailing draft rows are worth the extra expert-weight traffic they cause. Our week-1 go/no-go measurement on Granite-3.1-3B-A800M and Qwen3-30B-A3B came back no-go: deciding mid-pass cannot beat a rule that decides before the pass. The same measurements showed that MLX's MoE kernel charges for every (row, expert) pair rather than for each distinct expert, so our main deliverable is now a fused Metal MoE kernel whose verify cost follows distinct experts, with a study of its cross-threadgroup synchronization and memory-consistency litmus tests on Apple and NVIDIA GPUs.

Work Completed So Far

  • Speculative decoding pipeline in MLX with per-layer expert-routing logs; verify cost measured at 1–8 rows on both models (Aanya).
  • Go/no-go replay of 2,442 logged verify rounds: in-pass truncation does not beat a pre-pass rule (Aanya); acceptance-signal probes confirm it (Anya).
  • Pre-pass truncation running inside mlx-lm, +13% tokens/s over verifying all drafts on Granite (Anya).
  • Bit-identity of kept rows after truncation, measured on both models (Anya).
  • Metal litmus tests on the M5 (Anya); CUDA trace replay and litmus tests on an NCSA Delta A100 (Aanya).
  • MLX C++/Metal extension skeleton building and passing its tests; fused-kernel design fixed (Anya).
